Scope of work

What an ETL engagement covers

Pipeline builds

New ETL/ELT pipelines from source systems — SaaS APIs, transactional databases, files, event streams — into your warehouse.

Pipeline rescue

Stabilizing inherited pipelines after a handover failed or a key developer left: reverse-engineering, fixing, then documenting.

Modernization

Moving from legacy schedulers and hand-rolled scripts to maintainable, observable transformation stacks.

Data modeling

Dimensional and wide-table models designed for the questions your business actually asks.

Performance & cost

Tuning warehouse workloads and pipeline schedules so compute bills track value, not waste.

Observability

Freshness, volume, and schema checks with alerting — so you hear about breakage before your stakeholders do.

ETL/ELT pricing guide

What drives the total monthly cost of an ETL engagement — so you can budget before we ever get on a call.

Cost driverWhat it meansHow it moves the number
Number of sources Each source system (SaaS app, database, file feed) needs its own extraction and monitoring. More sources → more build and maintenance hours.
Data volume & frequency Hourly incremental loads cost more to operate than nightly batches. Higher frequency → more compute and on-call attention.
Transformation complexity Simple replication vs. layered business logic with tested models. Complex logic → more modeling and QA time.
SLA & coverage Business-hours support vs. NOC-style monitored coverage. Tighter SLAs → higher retainer.
Tooling Managed connectors vs. custom-built extractors; warehouse compute pricing. Managed tools trade license fees for engineering hours.

Engagements are scoped as a fixed monthly retainer after a short discovery. No surprise invoices.
