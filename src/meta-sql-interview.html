Analyst Round

What the Meta analyst SQL round tests (September 2026 refresh)

Updated September 2026 · 22 Meta-tagged challenges in the bank · 7 play free, 15 are Pro

Analyst-round SQL questions keep returning to six shapes: rank inside a group, accumulate across rows, compare a row with its neighbour, count who is still here, find the repeat, and filter by existence. Each family below lists every Meta-tagged challenge that drills it. All 22 run in the browser and show the solution once solved.

1 · Ranking and percentiles 5 challenges

DENSE_RANK versus ROW_NUMBER on ties, NTILE quartiles, PERCENT_RANK, and a median with no percentile function. Say which tie-break the question wants before you write it — that sentence is usually worth more than the query.

HardPro

Second Highest Salary per Department

DENSE_RANK inside PARTITION BY, so tied salaries share a rank; one-person departments drop out.
HardPro

Median Salary Without PERCENTILE

ROW_NUMBER plus COUNT(*) OVER (), then average the middle one or two rows.
HardPro

Salary Percentile Ranking

PERCENT_RANK over the whole company, rounded to two places.
HardPro

Fare Percentile Ranking

NTILE(4) into fare quartiles, with a name tie-break in the ORDER BY.
HardPro

Department Salary Percentile Buckets

NTILE(4) per department, then CASE to label each quartile.

2 · Running totals, rolling windows and share of total 3 challenges

The frame clause and the empty OVER (). A running SUM, a three-row rolling average, and a category's share of the whole without a second query.

HardFree

Cumulative Distinct Customers Over Time

First-order date per customer in a CTE, new customers per day, then SUM() OVER (ORDER BY date). The one Hard Meta-tagged challenge that plays free.
HardPro

3-Movie Rolling Average Revenue

AVG() OVER (… ROWS BETWEEN 2 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW), rounded.
HardPro

Revenue Share by Category (Window %)

SUM(SUM(total)) OVER () as the denominator — a window on top of a GROUP BY.

3 · Sessionization and gaps-and-islands 2 challenges

Compare a row with its neighbour. LAG finds the gap, a running SUM of the gap flags becomes the session id, and the ROW_NUMBER-difference trick turns consecutive dates into islands.

HardPro

Order Sessionization by Customer

LAG on order_date per customer, CASE for a 30-day gap, running SUM for the session number.
HardPro

Island Length Classification

Date minus ROW_NUMBER groups consecutive days; MIN, MAX and COUNT per island, then a CASE label.

4 · Retention, cohorts and active users 5 challenges

Count who is still here. Daily actives, customers active in two or more months, days since the last order, and a first-order-month cohort table.

HardPro

Daily Active Customers

COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) per order_date — DAU on an orders table.
MediumFree

Multi-Month Active Customers

JOIN, then HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT strftime('%Y-%m', order_date)) >= 2.
MediumFree

Customer Recency Analysis

MAX(order_date) per customer and julianday arithmetic for days since the last order.
HardPro

Customer Retention Cohort

MIN(order_date) month as the cohort, JOIN back to activity, count per cohort month and order month.
MediumFree

Quarterly Hiring Cohort Report

CASE on the month to build the quarter, GROUP BY year and quarter.

5 · Self-joins and repeat behaviour 3 challenges

Find the repeat. The orders table joined to itself on the customer with the date window in the ON clause, and the employees table joined to itself on id + 1.

HardPro

Detect Repeat Buyers Within 7 Days

orders o1 JOIN orders o2 on the customer, o2 strictly later, at most 7 days apart; DISTINCT the pairs.
HardPro

Self-Join: Repeat Orders Within a Week

The same shape carrying both products — watch for the duplicate pair.
HardPro

Consecutive IDs

JOIN employees e2 ON e2.emp_id = e1.emp_id + 1, same department.

6 · EXISTS, relational division and join aggregation 4 challenges

Filter by existence. Correlated EXISTS, the every-category relational-division question, and a LEFT JOIN with COALESCE so empty groups still show.

MediumFree

EXISTS vs IN: Departments with Top Performers

Correlated EXISTS on the same table, then AVG per department.
MediumFree

Departments With High Earners

EXISTS with a salary threshold; DISTINCT department names.
HardPro

Customers with Orders in ALL Categories

HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT category) = (SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT category) FROM orders).
MediumFree

Membership Tier Revenue Analysis

customers LEFT JOIN orders, COALESCE(SUM, 0), and a COUNT DISTINCT on each side of the join.

Interview Format

How Meta SQL Interviews Work

💻

The Format

45-minute screen with 2-3 SQL questions of increasing difficulty. In candidate reports, you write queries live in a shared editor such as CoderPad, with the interviewer following your approach in real time — not just the final answer.

🔎

What They Look For

Correct logic and complete results. Edge case handling (NULLs, empty groups, ties). Query optimization awareness — can you explain why one approach is faster? Clear communication of your thought process.

🎯

How to Prepare

Practice window functions heavily — 10 of our 22 Meta-tagged challenges (45%) use them. Use real datasets, not toy examples. Time yourself at 15 minutes per question. Talk through your approach out loud while writing.
