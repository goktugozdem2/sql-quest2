## Segment 1b — Reached checkout, and our button failed them (2)

Found 2026-08-05 while looking for more people like pupsiiik. These two are
not hesitation, they are a defect:

```
yisus      03:54:31 lifetime → 03:54:51 annual → 03:55:04 monthly
           → 03:55:11 dismissed        all three plans in 33 seconds
hrishi998  18:37:16 monthly  → 18:37:36 monthly AGAIN → 18:37:53 annual
```

Nobody comparison-shops by clicking checkout three times in half a minute, and
nobody clicks the same plan twice twenty seconds apart. yisus fired three
checkout clicks with no `modal_shown` between them and a `modal_dismissed` at
the end — one page session throughout, so the browser never navigated.

The mechanism was in the build they ran. On 2026-07-15 the handler recorded
`pro_checkout_clicked`, then hit an unguarded `JSON.parse` of localStorage,
then set `window.location.href`. If that parse throws, the handler dies after
the analytics call and before the navigation — so our funnel records a click
that could never convert, and their screen does nothing. Commit 90e315f
wrapped that read in try/catch on 07-17 as a side effect of the receipt-email
refactor. Fixed by accident, never noticed.

**Both drafts are apologies, not pitches.** They claim what is observable (the
page did not open, it was our fault, it is fixed) rather than the exact
mechanism, which is strongly indicated but not provable per-user.

### This segment is not "people who couldn't do SQL". It's where we sent them.

Three of the eight were handed a **Medium or Hard challenge as their first
one**. `puee` opened a window-function challenge six times. `sachin2468` was
given "Running Total Revenue" on day one. That is not a user problem.

Then the wider number, which is the real finding here:

| first challenge | difficulty | started there | first solve was that one | ratio |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **1 Class Survival Breakdown** | **Medium** | **210** | **51** | **24%** |
| 91 Your First Query | Easy | 94 | 69 | 73% |
| 100 GROUP BY Basics | Easy | 67 | 18 | 27% |
| 94 Comparison Operators | Easy | 25 | 9 | 36% |
| 93 Filter with WHERE | Easy | 22 | 18 | 82% |
| 92 Pick Your Columns | Easy | 4 | 2 | — |

**Challenge 1 is the front door — 210 people, more than twice any other — and
it is a Medium with the worst ratio of the lot.** Challenge 91, which is the
one actually written to be first, converts at 73%. Same funnel, 3x the
outcome. Challenge 1 also carries all 30 of the `challenge_errored` rows on
this list (11 people, 11 browsers), which is the column-name wording bug fixed
earlier today.

`100 GROUP BY Basics` is the second problem: 67 people were given GROUP BY as
their opening move and 27% got a first win from it. It is tagged Easy, and by
syntax it is — but it is the fourth concept a beginner meets, not the first.
Difficulty tags are not ordering.

**Read the ratio honestly.** `first_challenge_solved` records the challenge
where someone got their *first solve*, not "did they finish the one they
started". Someone who opens ch1, fails, then wins on ch91 counts as a ch1
start and a ch91 first-solve. So 24% is not a clean completion rate — it is
"of people who began here, how many had their first win here". The comparison
across challenges is apples-to-apples because the definition is identical for
every row, and a 3x gap does not come from definitional noise. Don't quote 24%
as a completion rate in a post.

**What this makes actionable, in order:**
1. Stop putting challenge 1 in the first slot. It is Medium and it is the
   single most-used entry point.
2. Look at what routes people to 1 vs 91 — 210 vs 94 is not an accident and
   nobody chose it deliberately.
3. Move `100 GROUP BY Basics` out of the opening set.

## How to read the replies

Do not average them. At this n the value is in the specific sentence, not the
distribution — the same reason §18 refuses to add an NPS score. Look for the
one that names a challenge, a screen, or a moment. That is a fix.

Log anything durable into `scripts/funnel-report.sql` §16 (what users actually
told us) so the next read inherits it.
