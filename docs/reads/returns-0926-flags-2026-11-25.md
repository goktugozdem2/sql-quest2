# Which 09-26 flag cost the returns? (read 2026-11-25)

Follow-up to `docs/reads/second-session-2026-11-25.md` (PR #382): day-0
solvers who come back within 7 days fell from 40–53% to 18–33%, with a step
at the first opens of 09-25/26, when six flags flipped in one commit
(`781ce126`, **2026-09-25 22:41 UTC**): `freeQuota`, `companySetGate`,
`deadlineOffer`, `goalWallEarly`, `mockDoor`, `firstScreenChallenge`. This
read narrows the batch down as far as the data allows. Nothing was reverted
or changed; read-only queries on `pro_events`.

**Short answer.** Four of the six can be set aside on the data: too few people
saw them, or (first screen) a randomised arm kept the old screen and fell just as
far. **`freeQuota` is the strongest suspect**, and the only flag that reaches
every free person (the "N of 10 free solves used" counter, the Coach strip
line, the rewritten free-tier copy on the site). The heaviest day-0 users, the ones who actually
hit the wall, fell the most (−39 pts). But **78% of the lost returns are
people who never met the wall on day 0**. If `freeQuota` is the cause for
them, it works through the framing ("a ten-question trial"), and nothing
here proves that. One randomised holdout settles it (section 5). It needs the
founder's written go.

## Method

Same definitions as #382. People by `aid`. First open = first `app_opened`.
Day-0 solver = at least one `challenge_solved` on the UTC day of the first
open. Returned = any non-landing event on a later UTC day, within 7 days of
the first open. Internal usernames, the internal aid, `metadata.bot` and the
three crawler viewports are excluded (one such row drops the whole person).
Periods: **A** = first opens 2026-08-17 → 09-25 22:45 UTC (n = 497 day-0
solvers, 44.3% returned), **B** = 09-25 22:45 → 11-18 (n = 494, 23.7%). B
ends 11-18 so every person has a full 7 days. The base query is at the end.

This reproduces #382's four-day buckets within 1–3 people per bucket
(09-21: 48.5%, n 99; 09-25: 18.3%, n 60).

**The step sits on the flip, to the hour:**

| First opens | Day-0 solvers | Returned |
|---|---|---|
| 09-15 → 09-22 | 133 | 51.1% |
| 09-23 → 09-24 01:50 | 27 | 44.4% |
| 09-24 01:50 → **09-25 22:45** (session-token prep and the goal gate already live) | 42 | 40.5% |
| **09-25 22:45** → 09-28 | 44 | 13.6% |
| 09-29 → 10-04 | 80 | 28.8% |

The session-token client prep (09-24 01:46) and the goal gate (on and off on
09-25, **2 `goal_gate_shown` rows ever**) come before the step and leave no
mark. The step lands on the deploy that carried the six flags. That deploy
also carried the free-tier copy rewrite (`3f853276`, part of `freeQuota`'s
story), India pricing (`regionalPrice`) and Checkout Sessions (dark).

## 1. `firstScreenChallenge`: randomised, and the old-screen arm fell too

`first_screen_assigned {arm}` is written once, on the first-run start
screen. The arm is a hash of the `aid` (`src/utils/first-screen.js`), so the
arms are comparable. First opens 09-26 → 11-17:

| Arm | Assigned | Returned (all assigned, ITT) | Day-0 solvers | Solvers returned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `quiz` (the old screen) | 524 | **13.7%** | 130 | 28.5% |
| `challenge` | 508 | **15.0%** | 214 | 21.0% |
| no arm (deep links etc.) | 2,793 | 2.7% | 150 | 23.3% |

- By intent-to-treat the challenge arm returns 1.3 points more than the quiz
  arm (95% CI −3.0 to +5.6). The arm did not cost returns.
- The solver-only gap (21.0 vs 28.5) is selection. The challenge arm turns
  more low-intent visitors into one-to-three-solve solvers (114 vs 55 at
  1–3 solves), and those return less in either arm.
- **The quiz arm is the old first screen, and its solvers return at 28.5%
  against 44.3% before the flip** (−15.8 pts, CI −24.7 to −6.9). The old
  screen fell with everyone else, so the first screen is not the cause.
  #99 shipping the challenge arm (11-21) needs no second look on this
  ground.

## 2. The everyone-flags: who was exposed, and how much it can explain

Exposure is measured on day 0 (before any return can happen), among B's 494
day-0 solvers. **Selection bias:** walls and asks fire on heavier users, and
heavier users return more in every period. So "exposed vs not" inside B is
not an effect estimate. The useful figure is the **ceiling**: even if every
exposed person had been stopped from returning, how far could that move the
population rate? The ceiling is exposed share × the A rate (44.3%).

| Flag | What it does | Exposed day-0 solvers in B | Returned exposed / not | Max share of the 20.6-pt drop it can explain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `deadlineOffer` | milestone ask leads with the date | **0**: `pro_modal_shown.deadline = true` has 0 rows ever (1,021 `false` since the flip; ledger V1 read 11-24 agrees) | — | **none** |
| `goalWallEarly` + `mockDoor` | interview-prep goal reorder and mock step | 6 (interview-intent Coach with a goal, or `goal_selected interview-prep`); 14 interview-prep goal picks site-wide since the flip | 33% / 24% | ≤ 0.5 pt |
| `companySetGate` | 4th of the Capital One / Revolut set walled | 16 (`wall='company_set'`) | 18.8% / 23.8% | ≤ 1.4 pt |
| `freeQuota`, the wall | 11th unsolved open walled | 47 (`wall='free_quota'`) | 27.7% / 23.3% | ≤ 4.2 pt as a wall alone |
| `freeQuota`, the counter and copy | "N of 10 free solves used" on the list header, the Coach strip's quota line, "your first 10 solves are free" on the pages | ~every free person (472 of 494 were guests at their first solve; all non-Pro) | (no unexposed group) | **all of it, in principle** |

Context rows (not flags): cold-start diversion 17 exposed. Mock tab or mock
start on day 0: 78 (29.5% vs 22.6%), so the mock door's own audience
returns *more*.

**Ruled out as a main cause on the data:** `deadlineOffer` (never fired),
`goalWallEarly` / `mockDoor` / `companySetGate` (each reaches ≤ 3% of
day-0 solvers and can move the rate by ≤ 1.4 pts),
`firstScreenChallenge` (section 1), `regionalPrice` (India-timezone solvers
42.3 → 22.0%, everyone else 45.7 → 25.1%: the same drop) and the goal gate
(2 shows).

## 3. The day-0 solve count: where the lost returns are

The quota wall only reaches someone at 10 solves, and the milestone ask
fires at 6. So the drop split by day-0 solve count separates "met a wall or
an ask" from "met neither".

| Day-0 solves | A n | A returned | B n | B returned | B met quota wall day 0 | B saw any Pro modal day 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 110 | 30.0% | 149 | 17.4% | 0% | 5% |
| 2–3 | 112 | 40.2% | 105 | 12.4% | 0% | 4% |
| 4–5 | 99 | 37.4% | 113 | 32.7% | 0% | 7% |
| 6–9 | 93 | 52.7% | 78 | 34.6% | 3% | 100% |
| 10+ | 83 | 67.5% | 49 | **28.6%** | 92% | 100% |
| all | 497 | 44.3% | 494 | 23.7% | 10% | 30% |

- **1–9 solves (no wall):** 39.6% → 23.1% (n 414 → 445), −16.5 pts, CI
  −22.6 to −10.4.
- **10+ (the wall):** 67.5% → 28.6% (n 83 → 49), −38.9 pts, CI −55 to −23.
  The ledger claim's guardrail was "returned-next-day among 10+ solvers
  down by no more than 10 points; > 15 points with < 2 purchases → revert".
  This 7-day measure is not that metric, but it points the same way. The
  claim's own read should say so.
- Mix is not the story. B's rates re-weighted to A's solve mix give 24.4%,
  against 23.7% as observed.
- **Counterfactual count:** at A's rates, B's 494 solvers would have
  produced ~203 returners; 117 returned, so ~86 were lost. Of those, **~19
  (22%) are the 10+ wall group and ~67 (78%) are people who never met the
  wall or the milestone ask on day 0.**

So the wall explains a fifth of the lost returns at most. The rest fell
among people for whom the only change in the batch was the quota's counter
and copy, unless the cause is something outside the batch that shipped in
the same deploy. Nothing in that deploy reaches everyone except the free-tier
copy, and that copy belongs to `freeQuota`.

Guests carry the drop: guests 43.7% → 22.9% (n 426 → 472), registered
47.9% → 40.9% (n 71 → 22, too few to read).

## 4. The payer side: did 09-26 buy anything?

`stripe_webhook` rows, internal accounts out, 30-day windows:

| Window | Purchases | Trials started | People who clicked checkout | People shown the modal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 06-28 → 07-27 | 3 | — | — | — |
| 07-28 → 08-26 | 1 | — | 3 | 157 |
| 08-27 → 09-25 | 2 | — (trial not live) | 7 | 259 |
| 09-26 → 10-25 | 2 | 4 | 27 | 248 |
| 10-26 → 11-24 | 2 (both trial conversions) | 5 | 16 | 127 |

- **Purchases did not move: 2 per 30 days before and after.** At this n,
  a change of one or two a month would not show.
- Checkout clicks rose from 7 to 27 people. But three checkout changes
  landed in the same weeks: the plan click straight to Stripe (09-23),
  Checkout Sessions in USD (09-30) and the 7-day trial (10-01). So the
  click rise cannot be credited to the flags.
- Where the money asks now come from: of the ~43 people who clicked
  checkout after the flip, **19 came from the `free_quota` wall**, 9 from
  `milestone_solves`, 2 from `company_set`. Of the 10 trial starts since,
  4 had touched a `free_quota` ask and 2 `company_set`. Of the 4
  purchases, 1 touched `free_quota` and 1 `company_set`.
- So `freeQuota` is the batch's best seller of clicks and trials, and the
  likeliest cost of the returns. That is the trade the test has to price.

## 5. The smallest test that settles it

**One flag, `freeQuota`: a 50/50 holdout by `aid` for new people only.**
`freeQuota` is a money/paywall flag. Any holdout needs the founder's
written go (CLAUDE.md; flag-queue rules). So do `companySetGate`,
`deadlineOffer`, `goalWallEarly` and `mockDoor`, but the data above gives
no reason to test those. `firstScreenChallenge` is the only non-money flag
of the six, and section 1 already clears it.

- **Arms:** `quota` (today) and `holdout` (the pre-09-26 behaviour: no
  wall, no "N of 10 used" counter, the old Coach strip line, the old
  in-app copy). Assigned once at the first `app_opened` of a new `aid`.
  People already on record keep the quota, so nobody gains or loses
  access mid-way. Hash with the murmur3 finalizer (the A/B hash trap in
  CLAUDE.md), stamped as `free_quota_holdout_assigned {arm}`.
- **Metric:** 7-day return of day-0 solvers, as defined here and in #382.
  Secondary: the same split into 1–9 and 10+ day-0 solves. That separates
  the wall from the framing. If 1–9 recovers in the holdout, the counter
  and copy are the cause.
- **n:** ~65 day-0 solvers a week, both arms together. A 20-pt gap (24% vs
  44%, the whole drop) needs ~85 per arm, about 3 weeks of enrolment. A
  10-pt gap needs ~320 per arm, about 10 weeks. **Proposal: 4 weeks of
  enrolment (~125 per arm, detects ≥ 16 pts at 80% power), read 7 days
  after enrolment closes.** If the founder's go comes by 2026-12-01:
  enrol 12-01 → 12-29, **read 2027-01-05**. The holidays affect both arms
  alike.
- **Decision rule, written before the data:** holdout − quota ≥ 12 pts
  (two-sided p < 0.05) → `freeQuota` cost the returns. Then weigh it
  against the purchases and trials the wall brings (expected cost of the
  holdout: about half the quota-sourced trials for 4 weeks, ~1–2 trials,
  ≤ 1 purchase). Gap ≤ 5 pts → it did not; the cause is outside the batch,
  and the next read is the 09-25 deploy's diff. In between, extend by 4
  weeks once.
- **Guardrail:** purchases and trials per arm (`stripe_webhook`, joined by
  username). Stop the holdout early only if it shows a dispute or refund
  tied to the arm.
- **Cheaper alternative, if the founder will not give up the wall:** keep
  the wall and hold out only the counter, the Coach strip line and the
  in-app "10 free" copy (50/50, same metric, 1–9 solvers only). It goes
  after the 78% and leaves the money path alone. It is still copy next to
  the paywall, so it still needs the go. A null result would leave the
  wall's own cost unpriced.

Nobody should revert any of the six on this read. Four are cleared, and the
fifth (`freeQuota`) carries most of the money signal and is unproven for
most of the lost returns.

## Base query

```sql
with e2 as (
  select created_at, event, reason,
         coalesce(((metadata #>> '{}')::jsonb)->>'aid', username) pid,
         (((metadata #>> '{}')::jsonb)->>'bot' is not null
          or coalesce(((metadata #>> '{}')::jsonb)->>'viewport','')
             in ('tablet:800x600','desktop:1280x1200','desktop:1919x992')) isbot,
         ((metadata #>> '{}')::jsonb) m
  from pro_events
  where created_at >= '2026-07-18' and created_at < '2026-11-28'
    and coalesce(username,'') !~* '^(test|qa_|fabletest|linktest|internalroutine|sqlquest$|elena$)'
    and coalesce(((metadata #>> '{}')::jsonb)->>'aid','') <> '363dfb2bc1314757acc88c8438c4d2ea'
),
p as (select pid, bool_or(isbot) b, min(created_at) filter (where event='app_opened') f
      from e2 group by pid),
c as (select pid, f, case when f < '2026-09-25 22:45' then 'A' else 'B' end per
      from p where not b and f >= '2026-08-17' and f < '2026-11-18'),
pp as (
  select c.pid, c.f, c.per,
    count(distinct x.m->>'challengeId') filter (where x.event='challenge_solved' and x.created_at::date=c.f::date) solves0,
    bool_or(x.event='content_lock_reached' and x.m->>'wall'='free_quota' and x.created_at::date=c.f::date) quota0,
    bool_or(x.event='content_lock_reached' and x.m->>'wall'='company_set' and x.created_at::date=c.f::date) cset0,
    bool_or(x.event='pro_modal_shown' and x.m->>'deadline'='true' and x.created_at::date=c.f::date) deadline0,
    max(x.m->>'arm') filter (where x.event='first_screen_assigned') arm,
    bool_or(x.reason is distinct from 'landing' and x.created_at::date > c.f::date
            and x.created_at <= c.f + interval '7 days') ret
  from c join e2 x on x.pid=c.pid
  group by c.pid, c.f, c.per
)
select per, count(*) n, round(100.0*avg(ret::int),1) pct_returned
from pp where solves0 > 0 group by per;
```

Swap the final `select` for the splits above: the arm (drop `solves0 > 0`
for ITT), `quota0` / `cset0` / `deadline0`, the day-0 solve buckets.
