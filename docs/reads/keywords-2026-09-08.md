# Are we playing the right long tail? — 2026-09-08

**Source.** GSC `sc-domain:sqlquest.app`, **last 28 days**, query breakdown with
clicks / impressions / CTR / position, read in the browser 2026-09-08. Top 110
rows collected. Site-wide for the window: **avg CTR 1.3%, avg position 17.5**.

This is a follow-up to `keywords-2026-09-07.md`, which did the demand and SERP
work. That analysis stands — one day changes nothing about demand. This one
asks a narrower question the other did not: **of the queries we are actually
being shown for right now, which ones are we winning, and are they the ones we
chose?**

## The one-sentence answer

**No. We are not playing a long tail — we are catching competitor brand
spillover.** The long tail we *do* own is small, specific, and converts
extremely well, which is the encouraging half.

## Where the clicks come from

194 clicks in 28 days across the collected rows.

| Bucket | Clicks | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **Our own brand** (`sql quest` 126 · `sqlquest` 30 · `query quest` 1 · `quest sql` 1) | **158** | **81%** |
| Everything else | 36 | 19% |

**Four out of five clicks are people who already knew our name.** Search is not
yet acquiring strangers; it is routing people who were coming anyway.

non-brand impressions or read nothing.**

## Three things worth acting on

**1. The company-page shape is proven, but not where we assumed.**
An earlier draft of this file said "seven of 23 company pages show up in GSC at
all". **That was wrong** — it was read off the query view, which hides anything
under ~8 impressions. The page breakdown shows **20 of 23 get impressions**.
The real finding is in the next section.

**2. Turkish demand is real and bigger than the query view showed.**

Corrected 2026-09-08 after pulling the page breakdown — the sentence that
stood here said we have "no Turkish landing page at all", which is **wrong**:

| Page | Clicks | Imp | CTR | Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/turkce-sql-ogren/` | 2 | 62 | 3.2% | 11.5 |
| `/blog/sql-join-nedir/` | 1 | 208 | 0.5% | 17.8 |
| `/blog/sql-cte-nedir/` | 0 | 72 | 0% | 28.8 |

**~342 impressions in 28 days across three Turkish pages**, not twelve. And
`/turkce-sql-ogren/` has a problem worth fixing on its own: it exists in
`public/` but **not in `src/`**, so `build-static-pages.js` never injects
`track.js` into it — the same tracking hole the `/challenges/*` pages had until
they were moved. It is ranking at 11.5 and we cannot see a single thing anyone
does on it.

**3. Stop chasing the head terms.** `sql group by` at 72.7, `group by in sql`
at 77.3, `joins in sql` at 54.0. LearnSQL, w3schools and DataLemur own these
with years of authority. The 09-07 doc already scored these as informational
mass; this read confirms the position reality behind that. Nothing we write
moves position 73 to page 1.

## Addendum: what is different about Revolut and Stripe

Asked directly, and the answer is **nothing about our pages**.

### It is not the content

The 23 company pages are one template. Revolut is 1,897 words and Stripe 1,915,
against a spread of 1,852-1,993 for the fifteen that convert worst — same six
H2s, zero FAQ schema, zero code blocks, zero challenge deep links. And the
pages we invested *most* in do *worst*:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| amazon | 3,150 | 34 | 0 | 1.8% | 18.0 |
| google | 2,876 | 12 | 2 | 1.6% | 9.8 |
| meta | 2,789 | 22 | 0 | 1.6% | 13.4 |
| **revolut** | **1,897** | **0** | **0** | **14.4%** | **7.7** |
| **wise** | **2,074** | **0** | **0** | **13.7%** | **8.2** |

### It is not the position either

`/shopify-sql-interview/` ranks **7.4** — better than Revolut's 7.7 — from
**307 impressions**, more than Revolut's 277. It converts at **1.6%** against
Revolut's **14.4%**. Same template, same position, more impressions, nine times
worse.

### It is who is searching

Filtering the query view to each page:

| `/revolut-sql-interview/` | | | `/shopify-sql-interview/` | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| revolut sql interview questions | 30% CTR | | shopify data science sql question leaked | 0% |
| revolut data analyst interview | — | | site:sqlquest.app | 0% |
| revolut analytical test | — | | | |

Everyone reaching the Revolut page is preparing to interview at Revolut. The
one identifiable person reaching the Shopify page wanted leaked answers.

Sorted by CTR, all 20 pages split cleanly:

| Converts (CTR ≥ 5%) | Does not (CTR < 2%, despite position 7-18) |
|---|---|
| revolut 14.4% · wise 13.7% · spotify 8.1% · snowflake 7.4% · stripe 5.5% · jpmorgan 5.3% | shopify 1.6% (pos 7.4) · google 1.6% (9.8) · apple 1.6% (10.8) · meta 1.6% (13.4) · amazon 1.8% (18.0) · airbnb 1.8% (16.7) · uber **0%** (12.8) |

**The losers are mass consumer brands whose "X SQL" query space is owned by
something else.** "google sql" is Cloud SQL and BigQuery. "amazon sql" is
Redshift and Athena. "shopify sql" is ShopifyQL and Shopify reports. We appear
for those queries and nobody wants an interview-prep page. The 09-07 read
already caught one instance without generalising it: *"`stripe sql` 154 imp is
Stripe Sigma intent — not ours."*

**The winners are mid-size fintech**, where "X SQL interview" has exactly one
plausible meaning: someone is interviewing there. Revolut, Wise, JPMorgan,
Plaid, Ramp.

Snowflake is the instructive exception — a data product whose name should
collide, but the query that reaches it (`snowflake sql practice`, 7.7% CTR at
6.3) is genuinely practice intent, so it converts anyway. The rule is not
"avoid data products", it is **does the query mean what our page answers**.

### What follows — and what does not

**Does not follow:** write sixteen more company pages. Nor invest more content
in the FAANG pages. Amazon has 3,150 words, 34 challenge links and 1.8% CTR at
position 18; the ceiling there is not editorial, it is that the query space
belongs to Redshift.

**Does follow:** the shape that works is mid-size fintech and finance, and it
works with our *plainest* template. Two of the three people who have ever paid
were preparing for Capital One — same shape.

**But do not pick the next names from this table.** The 09-07 read dropped
Goldman, Atlassian, Bloomberg and DoorDash for exactly the right reason: their
demand was invented. Before any new page, check that `<company> sql interview
questions` is a query people actually run, that the SERP has no dedicated
incumbent, and that the name does not collide with a data product. That is
three checks and they cost an hour; the page costs a day.

**Caveat on all of the above.** GSC anonymises rare queries: Revolut's page
shows 277 impressions but only 30 across queries it will name, Shopify 307
against 12. The page-level CTR is solid; the query-level attribution is a
visible slice of a mostly hidden tail. The direction is strong and consistent
across 20 pages, but it is inference, not a census.

## What this changes about the plan

Nothing in `keywords-2026-09-07.md` is wrong, but its ranked table is a
*demand* ranking. This read adds the *position* reality, and the two together
say: the cheapest wins are the pages already sitting at 3-12 (the comparison
and company set), not the ones sitting at 45-77 (the concept/informational
set), regardless of how much volume the latter has.

## Addendum 2026-09-09: a third thing inflating the impression count

Chasing the worst-converting page turned up a class the 09-07 read named but
this file had not folded in.

`/blog/null-handling-mistakes/` looked like the best available target: **906
impressions at position 11.4, converting at 0.2%.** Position 11 with 906
impressions should not produce two clicks, so it read as a title or snippet
problem — cheap to fix, unblocked, and pointed straight at O1.

It is neither. Its query breakdown over 90 days:

| Query | Imp | Position | Clicks |
|---|---|---|---|
| `"i also like the way they handle missing/null in predicates"` | **142** | 9.0 | 0 |
| null handling sql | 16 | 24.5 | 0 |
| `how are nillcrumtoz` | 6 | 6.7 | 0 |
| `rockset sql undefined null semantics` | 6 | 10.2 | 0 |
| `fail to reject the null` | 5 | 85.8 | 0 |
| `"stockunsuitabilityreasonsql must return three fields"` | 3 | 10.0 | 0 |

The top query is a **quoted sentence** — an exact-phrase search of the kind an
AI system runs when fanning out to find a source. `fail to reject the null` is
a statistics term with no SQL intent at all. `how are nillcrumtoz` is not a
word.

**These impressions are not people, and no title will make them click.** The
09-07 read had already measured the class — "16 quoted-sentence / AI fan-out
queries, 708 impressions, 0 clicks by construction" — but treated it as noise
to exclude rather than as a third systematic inflator sitting alongside the
competitor-brand mass.

### What this does to the numbers in this file

Three separate things inflate our impression count, and none of them can
click:

1. **Competitor brand navigation** — ~13,200 of ~17,000 in 28 days, 0.1% CTR
2. **AI fan-out on quoted sentences** — hundreds of impressions, 0% by
   construction
3. **Genuine mismatches** — `fail to reject the null`, `quest software sql
   navigator`, `shopify data science sql question leaked`

Whatever is left after those three is the real human search presence, and it
is **much smaller than any impression total we have quoted**. When O1's
traffic column is read on 2026-10-09, read signups and app opens — not
impressions.

### And a negative result worth keeping

The plan for finding the next company page was: look for company names in our
own GSC data that we do not yet have a page for, so the demand is measured
rather than invented. **That is structurally impossible.** GSC only reports
queries we already appear for, and we only appear for companies we have
already written about. Searching the top 230 queries of the last 90 days
returned no new company name — not because there is no demand, but because
this instrument cannot see it.

Finding the next name needs either an external volume signal or the SERP test
the 09-07 read used: count the dedicated pages competing for
`<company> sql interview questions`. If several sites wrote one, demand
exists. That test is available in a browser and costs an hour per name.
