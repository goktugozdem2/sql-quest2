## 2. The first-solve overload (context for `mobileOneLayer`, #282)

**What a person meets at the first solve today** (code on main plus the
events; the same on desktop and on a phone, only the layout differs):

| Layer | Evented? | Who meets it (28 days, first solvers) |
|---|---|---|
| Achievement toast | no | everyone (code) |
| Skillmap "Explorer" card (radar pop) | no | everyone (code) |
| LEVEL UP banner | no | when the XP crosses a level |
| Share bar | no | everyone (code, per #282's walk) |
| **Streak card, auto-open** | `streak_card_opened {source:'auto'}` | **202 / 205 desktop, 6 / 6 mobile, 3 / 3 tablet** — guests included; it waits for the radar and achievement toasts to clear, then slides in |
| Intent modal (3 s after the solve) | `intent_captured {source:'ask'}` on an answer | answered by 160 / 205 desktop, 6 / 6 mobile; 34 desktop arrived with an intent from a link and are not asked |
| Goal picker | `goal_picker_shown` | 9 desktop, 1 mobile |
| Pro / signup modal within 90 s | `modal_shown`, `pro_modal_shown`, `signup_prompt_shown` | 2 desktop |
| Company ask | `company_ask_assigned` | **0** — `intakeAfterFirstSolve` is off |

So the floor is **five to six layers for nearly everyone**: four unevented
ones, the streak card and the intent modal. #282's walk notes "a registered
user's streak card (the guest path never shows it)" as not walked; the data
says the guest path shows it to ~98% of first solvers within 90 s. #282
holds the streak card in arm `one`, so the arm covers it, but its walk
undercounts the control by one layer.

**Do more layers mean fewer second solves?** `second_solve_same_session`
(metrics.md definition, 28 days, first solves older than one hour):

| Band | First solvers | 2nd solve same session |
|---|---|---|
| desktop | 205 | 134 (65%) |
| mobile | 6 | 4 (67%) |
| tablet | 3 | 2 |

By the layers that leave a row (desktop; mobile n = 6 is not split):

| Group | n | 2nd solve same session |
|---|---|---|
| answered the intent modal | 160 | 112 (70%) |
| intent from a link, no modal | 34 | 20 (59%) |
| no intent at all (met the modal, never answered) | 11 | 2 (18%) |
| 0–1 evented layers | 42 | 22 (52%) |
| 2 evented layers (streak + intent) | 155 | 109 (70%) |
| 3+ evented layers (adds goal picker or a modal) | 8 | 3 (38%) |

- **No dose-response on desktop.** The 2-layer group does best; the 0–1
  group is mostly link-intent arrivals and non-answerers. The non-answerers'
  18% is reverse causation as much as cost: a person already leaving does
  not answer. Only the 3+ group (n = 8) points the way #282 expects.
- **Mobile cannot be read**: 6 first solvers in 28 days, all of them met
  the streak card and answered the intent modal; 4 solved again. That is
  #282's own small-sample rule (under 15 per arm is directional).
- What this means for #282: the overload hypothesis is not supported or
  refuted by desktop data, and mobile is too thin to say. The test stands on
  the walk (W3) and on its own arms, not on a pre-existing correlation.
  Four of the layers it changes emit nothing, so the test can be read only
  on the outcome metric.

**The drop between app open and first run is 393 of 631 people. More than
half of it (225, 57%) is inside the challenge page:**
- app open → no challenge opened: **168** (27%). The company door alone is
  60 of them (43% of its openers never open a question) — exactly what
  `companyDirectOpen` tests. `/sql-exercises/` loses 34, (no source) 23.

  pages open a challenge for 96% and still lose half here (110 → 58).

**What the non-runners did after opening their first challenge** (28 days,
first `challenge_opened` per person):

| Band | Opened, never ran | No event after the open | 20 s – 2 min | over 2 min | opened a hint | opened a 2nd challenge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| (runners, desktop) | 234 | 13 | 24 | 197 | 97 | 167 |

Three quarters of the people who open a question and never run it leave
**no row after the open**. The app records nothing between the open and the
run (no typing event, no leave event), so a person who read the prompt for
three minutes, typed and gave up looks the same as one who closed the tab in
two seconds. That is the single biggest unmeasured step in the funnel, and
the stuck ask (`stuckAsk`, dark until 12-20) only fires after 3 minutes on
screen. Signal raised (§6).

## 6. Signals raised

1. `exitReasonAsk` re-asks after every Pro modal close and counts answers
   above shown (§1). Bet: clear `fbPrompt` after a successful answer, give
   the thanks state a timeout, and read the claim on distinct people. S.
   E2's surface; the flag can stay on meanwhile (no money surface touched).
2. The open → run leak is mostly unmeasured (§4): 73% of desktop and 80% of
   mobile non-runners leave no row after opening their first question.
   Bet: B2 instruments one `challenge_left {secondsOpen, typed, visible}`
   on `pagehide` / hidden for an open with no run (rule 6b: its own PR,
   no behaviour change), so the next experiment on this step knows whether
   it is a bounce or a give-up. S.
3. The first-solve stack is five to six layers for nearly everyone, and
   the streak card auto-opens for guests too (§2). For #282's reviewer:
   the walk's control column is missing the streak card; no desktop
   dose-response exists to lean on. S (a review note, no new build).
